====================================================================
1. THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT DESIGN PRINCIPLE
====================================================================

Every major concept must answer five questions simultaneously:

WHAT IS IT?
HOW DOES IT WORK?
HOW DO I OPERATE IT?
HOW DOES IT AFFECT THE SURVEY?
HOW DO I PROVE THE RESULT IS TRUSTWORTHY?

A subject is incomplete until all five are represented.

Example:

COMPASS CALIBRATION

Not sufficient:
“Calibrate compass before flight.”

Required:
1. Show the magnetometer physically inside the aircraft.
2. Show X/Y/Z magnetic measurements.
3. Show Earth’s magnetic field vector.
4. Show hard-iron offset.
5. Show soft-iron distortion.
6. Show calibration data collection.
7. Show the fitted compensation model.
8. Show corrected output.
9. Move aircraft next to a steel excavator.
10. Show local disturbance despite prior calibration.
11. Show heading error.
12. Compare with an independent reference.
13. Let the learner diagnose the difference.
14. Explain calibration versus declination.
15. Explain calibration versus local attraction.
16. Explain why a “successful calibration” does not prove the environment is magnetically clean.

This depth rule applies everywhere.

257. MASTER GOLDEN PRINCIPLES
====================================================================

1. Understand the objective before choosing the equipment.
2. Understand the reference before trusting the coordinate.
3. Understand the height type before trusting the elevation.
4. Calibrate sensors according to applicable manufacturer procedure.
5. Calibration does not eliminate environmental disturbance.
6. Magnetic north is not true north.
7. Grid north is not automatically true north.
8. CRS metadata belongs with the coordinate.
9. GSD is a geometric relationship, not just a number in software.
10. Overlap creates the observational redundancy needed for image matching.
11. A keypoint is not automatically a tie point.
12. A tie point is not a GCP.
13. A GCP is not an independent checkpoint.
14. More points do not automatically mean more accuracy.
15. A visually beautiful model can still fail independent QC.
16. A point cloud is not automatically terrain.
17. A smooth DTM can be wrong.
18. Volume requires explicit surfaces, boundaries, reference/base and methodology.
19. Raw observations and metadata are evidence.
20. Every engineering result requires traceability.
21. Simulation is not certification.
22. Software is a tool; engineering judgement remains essential.

====================================================================

- understand whether terrain may exceed planned clearance
- understand aircraft-specific RTH logic.

Failure case:
RTH path crosses highwall/structure.

Learner must evaluate alternatives according to the aircraft and site procedure.
